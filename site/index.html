Managed barracks upkeep · Army installations

BRP keeps every barracks room livable and proves it.

One Barracks Operations Center runs room sensors, door QR reports, standard inspections and repairs for up to 20 installations. BRP crews will answer emergencies within 1 hour, and commanders get a room-by-room record they can sign.

01 · The problem

Inspected, scored and still unlivable

GAO found barracks that scored above 90 on condition ratings while rooms inside them were unlivable. The record says one thing and the room says another.

installations GAO visited had mold in barracks.

Soldiers moved out of moldy barracks at Fort Bragg in August 2022.

Sec. 2829, FY2026 NDAA

P.L. 119-60, signed December 18, 2025, creates a DoD "pilot program for emerging technologies for moisture control and mitigation" in certain military housing. The House bill with the same title, H.R. 6759, called for "sensor-based humidity or spore monitoring" at 3 to 5 installations.

CRS R48899, Apr 8, 2026
The Army's own sensors at Fort Stewart

The Army's Mold Conditions Awareness Tool (MCAT), built at Fort Stewart with Georgia Tech Research Institute, runs 4,200+ sensors in 87 barracks buildings. The Army reports mold work orders fell from more than 2,000 a year to about 700. That result belongs to the Army and GTRI, and no Army-wide rollout has been announced.

army.mil, Jul 24, 2026

Upkeep today is split across tenants, garrison DPW, contractors and barracks managers. BRP puts it under one program with one set of clocks and one record.

02 · How it works

Five parts run from one operations center

A sensor reading or a Soldier's photo becomes a ticket. The ticket becomes a repair, and the repair becomes part of the record.

  1. 01

    Sense

    Rev 1 room sensors watch temperature, humidity and dew point, and a pod tuned to each post adds wall moisture, supply air or pipe checks before anyone smells mold.

    • Room unit with a 2.9 in e-paper screen
    • Leak pucks and HVAC monitors
    • Private building Wi-Fi with cellular backhaul
    • No cameras or microphones
  2. 02

    Report

    Every door and every room screen carries a QR code. A Soldier scans it, takes a photo or short video and sends the report in about 30 seconds.

    • No app to download and no login
    • Room number fills in from the code
    • Status comes back by text message
  3. 03

    Inspect

    Barracks managers walk rooms with a tablet checklist that matches the DoD habitability standard line for line.

    • Matched to UFC 4-721-01, Appendix A
    • Photos attached to each failed item
    • Failed items open tickets automatically
  4. 04

    Fix

    BRP crews and vetted subcontractors will work one ticket per problem against the DoD clock.

    • Mirrored into the Army Maintenance Application (ArMA)
    • Stocked parts cabinets on post
    • Closed only with a photo and a passing re-check
  5. 05

    Prove

    Commanders see each room as it is and can send a report up the chain without rebuilding it.

    • Commander dashboard by building and room
    • Report formatted for Congressional inquiries
    • Every number traces back to a ticket or a reading
03 · Room sensor Rev 1

A room sensor Soldiers can read

The Rev 1 room sensor runs on two AA cells, joins a private building Wi-Fi 6 network and shows room status on an e-paper screen. It is a design concept and has not been built or tested.

  1. 1E-paper screen2.9 in, 296 × 128, mirrors status from the app
  2. 2Scan to reportRoom QR opens a prefilled work order
  3. 3Wi-Fi 6 and BLE radioSilicon Labs SiWG917, Austin, Tex.
  4. 4Isolated sensor chamberTI HDC3022 temp and humidity, ±0.5% RH typ
  5. 5Button and status lightRefresh or acknowledge
  6. 6Keyed pod connectorMold, Heat/AC or Freeze pod per post

Pod · Mold

BRP-RS1-M

Wall moisture

A TMP117 wall-contact probe lets the unit compute humidity at the coldest wall and alert at 80% surface RH (ISO 13788). Example posts: Stewart, Bragg, Benning, Hawaii, Korea.

Pod · Heat/AC

BRP-RS1-H

Supply air

A probe on the supply register flags an AC failure before the room heats up. Example posts: Bliss, Hood, Irwin, Polk.

Pod · Freeze

BRP-RS1-F

Pipes and leaks

A pipe-clamp probe and leak contacts alert below 40°F or when water appears. Example posts: Drum, Carson, Wainwright, Riley.

Part numbers: BRP-RS1-B room unit, BRP-RS1-M, -H and -F pods, BRP-RS1-GW building gateway. These are BRP designations for a design that is not yet built, with no NSN assigned. See the pod for your post.

    05 · How it connects

    From the room to a U.S. government cloud

    Each building gets its own private Wi-Fi network and a cellular gateway. Data leaves over cellular, so nothing plugs into the base network.

    1. 1 · Rooms

      Room sensors and leak pucks

      Room units and leak pucks run on batteries. Leak pucks report to the room unit over BLE, and the room unit reports over Wi-Fi 6. Readings buffer on the unit if Wi-Fi drops.

    2. 2 · Building

      Private Wi-Fi 6 network

      A private network per building with WPA3 and per-device keys, on an isolated VLAN. Devices can reach only the BRP message broker.

    3. 3 · Cellular

      Gateway backhaul

      The building gateway sends data out over its own encrypted cellular link with no public address.

    4. 4 · Cloud

      U.S. government cloud

      AWS GovCloud, run by U.S. persons, with a target of IL4 and FedRAMP High. Dashboards and room pages are served from there.

    Never on the Army network

    BRP hardware stays off the garrison LAN, base Wi-Fi, NIPR and every Army-managed network, which keeps it outside the installation's ATO boundary. Gateway network ports are switched off and tamper-labeled.

    06 · Zero lift for the garrison

    We install it, train on it and run it

    The installation stands up no new IT system, sponsors no network accreditation and reassigns no staff. Its job is to give us access to the buildings.

    1. 1Step 1 · Survey

      We walk every building

      A baseline inspection of each room to the Appendix A checklist, plus a map of HVAC, water and moisture risk and a Wi-Fi site survey.

    2. 2Step 2 · Install

      We put in the hardware

      Room sensors, leak pucks, the building gateway, door QR tags and parts cabinets. The tablet app commissions each sensor to its room over BLE.

    3. 3Step 3 · Train

      We train the people

      Short sessions for barracks managers and unit leaders, and a one-page card for Soldiers. Scanning a door code needs no training.

    4. 4Step 4 · Operate

      We run it every day

      The Barracks Operations Center watches readings, routes tickets, sends crews and keeps ArMA current.

    • Nothing connects to the garrison network
    • No software for the installation to install or host
    • No new accounts for Soldiers to create
    • Work orders still show up in ArMA, where staff already look
    07 · Who it serves

    The Soldier in the room and the commander who signs for it

    For Soldiers

    Report a problem once and get updates by text

    • Scan the code on your door or room screen with your phone camera.
    • Take a photo or short video, pick what's wrong and send. It takes about 30 seconds.
    • Get a text when the ticket is assigned, scheduled and fixed.
    • Emergencies such as a leak or no heat go to the top of the queue.

    For commanders

    See every room and show the record up the chain

    • One dashboard for every building, with each room's status and any open tickets.
    • Tickets tracked against the DoD response and resolution clocks, with misses flagged.
    • Humidity trends that show a problem forming weeks before a mold complaint.
    • A ready report for the chain of command and Congressional inquiries.
    • One accountable vendor, so nothing falls between tenant, DPW and contractor.
    08 · The DoD standard clocks

    We work to the clock the standard already sets

    Every ticket starts a timer when it opens. The dashboard shows how many closed inside the window and which ones missed it.

    PriorityRespondResolveExamples
    Emergency1 hr24 hrsActive leak, no heat in winter, no AC in extreme heat, life-safety issue
    Urgent8 hrs7 daysVisible mold growth, failed exhaust fan, broken lock
    Routine24 hrs30 daysWorn flooring, damaged blinds, loose fixtures

    Priority categories and timeframes follow DoD barracks habitability guidance (UFC 4-721-01, Appendix A). Urgent and Routine respond times are acknowledgment times. Example issues are illustrative; the installation's own priority list governs.

    Long-lead parts promise

    If a part is more than 7 days out, the room does not wait

    We use one of four options so the room stays livable until the permanent part arrives.

    • SubstituteAn equivalent part that is in stock and fits the equipment.
    • ExpediteFaster shipping or a different supplier with the part on hand.
    • Local fabricationMade locally for non-safety parts only, never for life-safety, fire or gas components.
    • Interim fixA temporary measure, such as a portable dehumidifier or a capped line.

    From report to fix

    1. Minute 0

      Soldier reports

      Scans the door code and sends a photo or short video in about 30 seconds.

    2. Within 8 hrs

      Barracks manager verifies

      Confirms the problem and its priority, or adds what the photo missed.

    3. Within 24 hrs

      Contractor accepts or inspects

      The repair crew takes the ticket, or walks the room first if the cause is unclear.

    4. Same step

      Parts order placed automatically

      If a part is needed, the order goes out from the ticket with no separate request to chase.

    5. On shipment

      Tracking attached

      Shipment tracking is added to the ticket, so the Soldier and the manager see when the part arrives.

    6. DoD clock

      Fixed on time

      Emergency within 24 hours, Urgent within 7 days, Routine within 30 days.

    7. After the fix

      Soldier confirms

      The Soldier gets a text and confirms the fix from the same door code. The ticket closes with a photo and a passing re-check.

    Emergencies such as an active leak or no heat in winter go straight to the crew with a 1-hour response.

    Room Experience Score

    The score holds even when few people tap

    BRP combines one-tap votes with signals people create without answering anything. Commanders see the score by building and by month, linked in BRP One to the rooms, work orders, technicians, vendors and parts behind it.

    • One-tap votesFixed, OK or Not fixed at close, plus wall-button answers.
    • Reopen rateWork orders reopened after a close.
    • Repeat reportsThe same problem reported again in the same room within 30 days.
    • Time to confirmHours from close to the Soldier's confirmation.
    • Complaint wordsWords such as mold, smell, leak or cold in free-text reports.
    • Monthly pulseThe 5-face answer, counted by building.

    Anonymous by default

    Monthly pulse answers carry no name. A rating on a specific work order carries the Soldier's name so the crew can follow up.

    No incentives

    BRP offers no gifts, drawings or rewards for answers, in keeping with government ethics rules.

    One question at a time

    A Soldier sees one question at most at any moment, and never a multi-page form.

    11 · Pricing model

    We get paid when rooms are livable

    BRP works on a cost-plus contract with open-book costs. Our fee is earned per habitable room-day, so we make money only on rooms Soldiers can live in.

    How the fee is calculated

    Fee = habitable room-days × rate per room-day

    A habitable room-day is one room for one day that meets the standard, with no open Emergency ticket and no overdue Urgent ticket.

    Costs at cost

    Labor, parts, sensors and subcontractors are billed at actual cost with full records.

    Fee tied to outcome

    A room out of standard earns no fee that day. Fast repairs are how we protect our margin.

    Same data on both sides

    The room-day count comes from the same tickets and inspections the government sees.

    12 · Compliance and trust

    Built to the rules the government already has

    Where we meet a requirement, we say so. Where we are working toward one, we write "designed to" or "path to." BRP holds no certifications today.

    Design rule

    U.S.-hosted data

    Data stays in the United States on AWS GovCloud. The cloud provider holds the FedRAMP authorization; BRP's system is designed to inherit its controls, with a target of IL4.

    Design rule

    NDAA Sec. 889, 5949 and 1260H

    No Huawei, ZTE, Hytera, Hikvision or Dahua parts or services, no chips from SMIC, CXMT or YMTC, and every supplier checked against the 1260H list before each buy. No PRC radio modules anywhere.

    Where required

    Buy American and TAA

    Final assembly in the U.S. The e-paper panel is from Taiwan, a TAA-designated country, and is documented as the one non-U.S. part.

    Path to

    CMMC Level 2

    We are building our security program toward CMMC Level 2 and will report our status plainly as it progresses.

    Design rule

    Privacy Act aware

    We store a phone number to text status back. We do not collect or store Soldier personnel records, and the hardware carries no cameras or microphones.

    Designed to

    Section 508 and WCAG 2.1 AA

    The Soldier report page, the dashboard and this site are designed to meet WCAG 2.1 AA, with checks against 2.2 AA: readable contrast, full keyboard use, labeled fields and reduced motion.

    American and veteran-founded

    BRP is a veteran-founded U.S. program team. Our goal is American-made hardware wherever a qualified U.S. maker exists, and U.S. crews on every post. We say which parts meet that goal and which do not yet.
